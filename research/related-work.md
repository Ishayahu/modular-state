# Related work: preliminary map

**Status:** preliminary, not a novelty claim.

The current model has obvious relatives in several established traditions. This file is a map for deeper research, not a completed literature review.

## Polycentric governance

### Vincent Ostrom / Elinor Ostrom

Relevant themes:

- multiple autonomous decision centres;
- overlapping jurisdictions;
- cooperation and competition;
- governance without assuming one monolithic hierarchy;
- commons governance.

Questions for comparison:

- Does the literature define the decision-making demos endogenously from project impact?
- How are cross-centre enforcement and finality handled?
- How much permanent higher-level structure is retained?

## FOCJ

### Bruno S. Frey / Reiner Eichenberger

Functional, Overlapping and Competing Jurisdictions are especially close to the idea that different functions need not share one territorial jurisdiction.

Questions:

- membership and exit;
- taxation / compulsory contributions;
- functional boundaries;
- overlapping jurisdiction conflicts;
- whether FOCJ depend on a state-level constitutional shell.

## Panarchy / non-territorial governance

### Paul-Émile de Puydt

Relevant themes:

- political affiliation not determined exclusively by territory;
- coexistence of multiple governance providers;
- exit without physical relocation.

Questions:

- does panarchy select an entire government as a package, whereas this model decomposes functions?
- how are unavoidable shared resources handled?
- how are non-contractual conflicts handled?

## Polycentric law / private law / private governance

Relevant authors and traditions to investigate include:

- Bruce L. Benson;
- David D. Friedman;
- private arbitration;
- merchant law / lex mercatoria;
- legal pluralism;
- competitive legal systems.

Questions:

- private enforcement;
- inter-system conflict;
- collusion;
- protection of non-subscribers;
- public goods;
- monopoly of force.

## Club theory and Tiebout-style competition

Relevant for:

- voluntary membership;
- provision of local collective goods;
- exit;
- fiscal competition;
- sorting among governance packages.

Need to test whether these models require stronger territorial assumptions than the current proposal.

## Subsidiarity / functional federalism

Relevant for assigning functions to the smallest or most appropriate unit.

Difference to investigate: the current model attempts to derive the affected demos from the consequences of each project rather than from a fixed hierarchy of territorial units.

## Social choice and voting

### Approval voting

The current working procedure for multiple competing projects is approval voting: participants may approve more than one acceptable alternative.

Need to investigate:

- strategic properties;
- ties;
- relation to Condorcet and majority criteria;
- whether another method better matches the model's concept of acceptability.

## Temporary coalitions / confederations

A particularly important mechanism in the current model is:

> independent systems may aggregate a temporary function without creating a new permanent organisation that owns the participating systems.

Related bodies of work to investigate:

- confederal military arrangements;
- mutual-aid systems;
- temporary coalitions;
- joint task forces;
- federated incident response;
- Byzantine/distributed-systems analogies, with caution about overextending technical metaphors.

## Potentially distinctive combination to test

No originality claim is currently made.

The combination that requires the most careful prior-art search is:

1. decision-specific demos derived from reasonably foreseeable impact;
2. whole-project justification as both the object of consent and the basis for identifying the demos;
3. robust treatment of multiple credible scientific impact models through an AND rule;
4. competing legal/judicial/executive systems;
5. temporary aggregation of coercive capacity as a function rather than a permanent sovereign organisation;
6. voluntary exit with survival of prior property and contractual relations.

The research task is to determine whether this combination already exists under another name, whether some components are incompatible, and what criticism the relevant literatures already contain.
